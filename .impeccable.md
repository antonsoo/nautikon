## Design Context

### Users

The owner's brief specifies English-speaking learners of ancient Greek and ancient law, with interactive visual explanations and original-language reading. Assume no financial or Greek background. Readers should be able to change one voyage fact and follow its consequence back to a clause.

### Brand Personality

Exact, curious, maritime. Use concrete questions and plain English. Let the Greek do intellectual work. Never present a court simulator or a modern lending recommendation.

### Aesthetic Direction

A shipping dossier: bone paper, deep marine ink, vermilion event markers, ruled accounts and a continuous voyage diagram. This direction is a documented design choice under the owner's overnight instruction to make sensible decisions. Large literary type, compact sans-serif controls, no decorative dashboard cards. The ship and cargo must carry the explanation rather than supply atmosphere.

### Design Principles

- Put the altered fact beside the resulting ledger entry and its Greek clause.
- Draw event order explicitly; star-relative days are illustrative rather than a historical calendar.
- Distinguish debt, goods under security and collection from other property.
- Retain uncertainty as a visible result, especially loss at sea and the Hellespont exception.
- Keep controls, source pages, exports and comparison usable by keyboard and at 375 px.
